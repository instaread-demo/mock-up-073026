Fair Lawn Resident Publishes Debut Novel: Fantasy Book Inspired by Jewish Values

  • By Adam Samuel
  • | July 24, 2026
Listen to audio version of this article

Highlighting: “Born of Broken Chains,” by Sam Larson. Everblade Press. August 2026. 368 pages. ISBN-13:‎ 979-8995884712.

Author Sam Larson

 

Sam Larson has been developing a story for nearly two decades. Growing up in a family of writers, he spent countless hours immersed in fantasy novels and began writing when he was just 15. Through high school, yeshiva, college and even after becoming a school psychologist, Larson never stopped returning to the page, revisiting and refining the same core story that first captured his imagination.

 

“Writing was a creative hobby for me,” Larson shared with The Jewish Link. “I would write here and there when inspiration struck. I eventually had a novel written by the time I was in YU, but I deleted about 80% because it wasn’t that good. Only about five years ago did I seriously commit to finishing and making it the best it could be.”

And so, while balancing a full-time job, married life and two young children, the Fair Lawn resident wrote during his commute to work and summer breaks. Now, it has finally culminated in “Born of Broken Chains,” Larson’s debut young-adult fantasy novel. The story follows Soren, a 17-year-old who escapes years of enslavement to a conquering empire and vows to free those he left behind, drawing him to an enigmatic mentor with a disturbing past and a rising rebellion. Alongside another teenage protagonist, Allia, Soren must navigate war, trauma, sacrifice and the price of power as magic begins to reawaken after centuries of absence.

 

“I’ve always loved fantasy because it gives you so much freedom,” Larson said. “You get to create your own world, its history, its cultures—really anything you can imagine. I write young adult fantasy because those stories are often centered on questions of identity and character. They’re about people figuring out who they are, what kind of person they want to become, and what they’re willing to sacrifice to get there. I think that makes for incredibly compelling storytelling.”

 

Larson credits the fantasy novels he grew up reading with inspiring the world of “Born of Broken Chains.” Authors such as J.R.R. Tolkien and Christopher Paolini particularly sparked his imagination as a young reader. And while the novel takes place in a fictional world, many of its themes such as resilience, hope, redemption, and the dangers of wielding power and violence were shaped by Larson’s Jewish upbringing and education.

 

“Judaism has always been a source of inspiration for me,” the author said. “My long-term goal is to write a Jewish ‘Lord of the Rings’ type story that can be enjoyed by anyone, but has uniquely Jewish elements and teachings under the surface.”

 

Although the story has been years in the making, Larson said the final push toward publication began only recently. Since finishing the first draft two years ago, he’s focused on polishing the manuscript, gathering feedback from readers, and preparing for every aspect of launching a book.

 

“I find revising what I’ve already written easier than creating the first draft. I enjoy the feeling that every round of feedback is making the story stronger.”

 

While “Born of Broken Chains” marks Larson’s debut as a published novelist, it is only the first book in a series that he’s planned to span three or four books. In fact, work on the sequel is already underway. For now, Larson is enjoying the realization of his teenage dream.

 

“I love it when readers ask me questions about my world or my characters,” Larson went on to say. “When readers share their reactions to plot points in the novel, it’s almost like I can experience the story for the first time again, which is a lot of fun.”

 

“Born of Broken Chains” will be released on Aug. 2 and is available for preorder on Amazon. To learn more about Larson, his upcoming projects, or to sign up for updates, visit samlarsonauthor.com. You can also follow him on Instagram @samlarsonauthor.

 

Adam Samuel is a journalist from Teaneck. He blogs at www.adamssoapbox.com.
