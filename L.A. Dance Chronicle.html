Europeans in pointe shoes pirouette in Costa Mesa and Downtown LA; Chinese dance meets Street dance in Laguna Beach; adventurous contemporary in Lincoln Heights, Frogtown, and Downtown LA Arts District; puppets go rogue in Hollywood; dance film pop-up in Downtown LA Arts District; plus more SoCal dance this week, and a peek at next week.

Live Performance This Week (ending August 6)

Hugo who?

Whether on a fashion runway, magazine photoshoot, or his main job as an Étoile (principal dancer) onstage at the Paris Opera Ballet, Hugo Marchand draws attention. While his fame extends beyond ballet in France, Marchand starts to raise his ballet profile here with Ballet Now, an assemblage of European ballet dancers performing under the banner Superstars of Paris. The dancers arrive after performing in New York with reviews mostly rapturous with a few quibbling at particular items on the mixed bill being more “past”, than “now.” Still, the dancers represent the cream of this generation. Also, the dance card here lists mostly different works from New York–gala staples like The Dying Swan and Roland Petit’s Carmen Pas de Deux, as well as 20th century ballets like George Balanchine’s Sonatine (1975), Hans van Manen’s Trois Gnossiennes (1982), and the lip-lock kiss pas de deux from Angelin Preljocaj’s Le Parc (1994). A full list of likely repertoire is at the website along with video clips of splendid dancers LA seldom sees live. The Music Center, Disney Hall, 111 S. Grand Ave., Downtown LA; Fri., July 31, 7:30 pm, Sat., Aug. 1, 7:30 pm, Sat.-Sun., Aug. 1-2, 2 pm, $36-$163. Ballet Now: Superstars of Paris.

This year’s MovementFest LA present its Performance Lab Showcase pairing LA-based Jobel Medina with the duo Rebecca Laufer (American) and Mats Van Rossum (Netherlands) who work in Europe. The event reflects the process and results from the programs’ three week residency. Info on this and other upcoming events/performances at the website. At LA Dance Project Studios, 3325 E. Washington Blvd., Downtown LA Arts District; Fri.-Sun., July 31 – August 2, 8 pm, $40-$75. Performance Lab.

Presented by MashUP Contemporary Dance, the quarterly showcase, Choreography Open Mic Night, returns. At the Pickle Factory, 2828 Gilroy St., Frogtown; Sat., Aug. 1, 8 pm, $15. Choreography Open Mic Night.

When puppets improv

The son of Muppets creator Jim Henson, takes his dad’s puppet concepts into a cast of rebellious puppets, crosses them with The Groundlings-style improv, adds a large dose of uncensored Avenue Q sensibility, then puts the brew into the hands of masterful Henson Company puppeteers. The result is Brian Henson Presents – Puppet Up! Each distinctive show includes improv driven by audience suggestions along with recreated classic Henson sketches, some not seen in decades. Recommended for age 16 and older (actually those under age 16 are not admitted because of where the improv may veer). At the Montalbán Theatre, 1615 Vine St., Hollywood; Fri., July 31 & Aug. 7, 8 pm, Sat., Aug. 1 & 8, 3 & 7 pm, Sun., Aug. 2 & 9, 5 pm, $71.70-$87.10. Puppet Up!

Trading camera for stage

Commercial choreographers are usually in service to others and having their moves be sliced and diced by editors for music videos, advertisements, television, and film. Choreographer’s Carnival allows uninterrupted stage time for choreographers to show their creative side and maybe audition for their next commercial gig. At the Vermont Hollywood, 1020 N. Vermont Ave., East Hollywood; Thurs.- Sun., July 30-Aug. 2, 9:30 pm, $31.86-$43.87 (includes fees). Choreographer’s Carnival.

The screening includes a ‘talk back’ session with the filmmakers. At LA Dance Project, 2245 E. Washington Blvd., Downtown LA Arts District; Wed., July 29, 8 pm, $22.50 – $38.50. LA Pops Up.

Send in the last clowns

Created and directed by Hank Jacobs, who also acts as the “ringmaster,” The Great Clown Bank Show is described as a “burlesque on income inequality in 15 circus acts.” Promising a blend of dance and circus, including a clown pas de deux, the show will include a weekly guest circus act. At the Open Fist Theater Company @ the Atwater Village Theater, 3269 Casitas Ave., Atwater Village; Fri., 9 pm, Sat., 8 pm, Sun., 7 pm, thru Sat., Aug. 1, $35.25, $31.25 seniors, $22.25 students. The Great Clown Bank Show.

Southbound escape

Choreographer Tony Thomas provides the moves as the award-winning hip-hop musical Mexodus unearths the story of a southbound Underground Railroad that led 4,000 to 10,000 enslaved people to seek freedom in Mexico. The two-person show began at Berkeley Repertory Theater which also nurtured Ain’t Too Proud, the hit Motown musical about The Temptations. Mexodus moved east after highly successful runs, including off-Broadway where it picked up impressive attention. Thomas is the choreography part of the artistic team led by creators/performers Brian Quijada and Nygel D. Robinson and director David Mendizábal. At the Pasadena Playhouse, 39 S. El Molino Ave., Pasadena; Thurs.-Fri., 8 pm, Sat., 2 & 8 pm, Sun. 2 pm, thru Sun., Aug. 2, $40-$127. Mexodus.

A two-fer

This annual gala for the respected training company, Festival Ballet Theatre, has a professional performance preceded by a performance by promising pre-professionals. The professionals announced for the 17th annual Gala of the Stars include Constantine Allen (Dutch National Ballet), Braylong Browner, Alessandro Frola (Vienna State Opera), Nikolas Gaifullin (Louisville Ballet), Peter Hull (Stuttgart Ballet), Maria Khoreva (Mariinsky Ballet), Paloma Livellara (Vienna State Opera), Kloe Walker (New York City Ballet), Madison Young (Vienna State Opera), and Andres Zuniga (New York City Ballet). The earlier performance features ten pre-professional protégés. At Segerstrom Concert Hall, 600 Town Center Dr., Costa Mesa; Thurs., Aug. 6, Inspired Young Proteges-6 pm, Gala of the Stars– 7 pm, $101.50-$169.50. Festival Ballet Theatre.
