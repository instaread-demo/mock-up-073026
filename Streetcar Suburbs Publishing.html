Duvall Field reopens after renovations

AYLIN AARHUS
July 27, 2026
JONATHAN PETER BELLING
Duvall Field Day on July 31 will feature a ribbon cutting, games and music as the long-planned park opens to the public.

After more than 20 years of planning, the newly renovated Duvall Field is ready to open, with a ribbon-cutting ceremony set for July 31.

The grand opening, billed as Duvall Field Day, will include speeches from city officials, along with a ribbon cutting, games and live music.

“This is a major, major project,” said Mayor Fazlul Kabir, “both in terms of the amenities we have put in, and also the money and the investment we are putting in.”

Duvall Field, located at 9119 Rhode Island Ave., was built in the 1960s and has been renovated multiple times, including in 2017 with the addition of a concession stand, bathrooms and a plaza area.

The recently completed renovation broke ground in March 2025 and included additions like pickleball, bocce and basketball courts, baseball and soccer fields, and exercise equipment. The cost for the final phase of the project was $7.95 million, funded by the city and through state programs that help finance construction and renovation of parks and playgrounds, according to the city.

The renovations initially were proposed in 2005, but the project lacked funding. In recent years, the COVID-19 lockdown delayed renovations even further, along with issues with stormwater drainage, Kabir said.

College Park City Councilmembers said they’re excited to see the field reopen.

Councilmember Kelly Jordan (District 2) said the newly renovated field will be “wonderful for the residents” of her district.

“It’s now going to be much more multipurpose with lots of built-in ways for the residents to come out and play, and it’s a beautiful space right here in College Park,” Jordan said.

Councilmember Alan Hew (District 1) said residents are interested in using the field for a wide variety of sports, from baseball to cricket to Frisbee.

Hew said the field will be “a new hub for young people and residents to utilize in a way that it hadn’t been used before.”

“Imagination is the limit,” Hew said. “This vibrant activity really completes and creates a community worth living in.”

A number of organizations hold team practices and events on Duvall Field, including several local churches. 

Officials said they are interested in holding more community events at the park, such as movie nights and live music performances, as well as celebrations organized by the city government. For instance, Duvall Field is set to host Bailamos!, College Park’s celebration of Hispanic Heritage Month, on Sept. 18.

Kabir said he is looking forward to playing soccer on Duvall Field and suggested a friendly game between city officials and staff.

“I still have all my gear and the cleats,” Kabir said. “So I’ll bring them out.”

Kabir said Duvall Field is important to bringing the College Park community together.

“It is all about community involvement, and so that they can have a good time together year-round,” Kabir said.

City Councilmember Jacob Hernandez (District 1), said the renovated park represents more than a good time, however.

“It’s a strategic investment in the future of College Park,” he said. “That’s the kind of strategic planning that maximizes taxpayer investments and strengthens our local economy while enhancing quality of life for everyone.”
