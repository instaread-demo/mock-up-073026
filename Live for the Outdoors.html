The ultimate family camping checklist – use this packing list to make your next trip easier

Packing is one of the most stressful parts of any family camping trip. To make it run smoother, here's our tried-and-tested family camping checklist

@LFTO/Tom Bailey

There’s a lot to love about family camping – getting the kids into nature, spending time away from screens, marshmallows around a fire. What none of us love, however, is the packing, which can feel like a stressful game of Tetris.

You can attribute the pain of packing to a lot of things. Getting everything into the car is always a mega challenge (in which case, you may want to invest in a roof box).

Making sure you don’t forget anything important can also be tricky, with many family camping trips involving an emergency dash to the shop mid-weekend for something that you forgot.

And then there’s the important task of picking the right kit for your trip. We’re talking appropriate layers and sleeping bags for the weather conditions, games for porch-based fun if it’s raining and the right cooking kit for your family feasts.

All this is a complex business, so to make it easier we’ve teamed up with GO Outdoors to bring you the ultimate family camping kit list, which you’ll find below.

This is just one part of our Family Adventure Week, running from 27 July to 2 August, where we’re sharing practical advice and inspiration for getting outdoors with your family. Keep an eye on the LFTO Instagram page and LFTO Facebook page for more advice.

Family camping checklist – what to take

Family tent

The obvious one and the biggest thing you’ll be packing into your car – a family tent. Pick one that sounds bigger than you need (e.g. if there’s four of you, go for a five-person tent), think about how much living space you want and pay close attention to pack size, and how that’ll fit into your car.

Tent accessories

Electric hook-up is a handy addition if you want to run a camping fridge or use an electric kettle or toaster. Duct tape can be a handy rescue remedy if your tent (or any other kit) gets damaged.

A mallet is another essential for knocking in pegs, especially when the ground is hardened due to lack of rain.

Sleeping kit

© lfto/tom bailey

A decent sleeping mat or airbed is vital to a good night’s sleep. These can range from a basic flock airbed to something more substantial like this Berghaus Air Double Self-Inflating Mat (which has 38%* off) gives you memory-foam-like comfort and year-round warmth. Just don’t forget a pump!

Sleeping bags are also key. Sure, you can take a duvet from home, but they’re far bulkier and less thermally efficient. You can opt for a double sleeping bag or a single sleeping bag, though if you have young kids, we’d recommend taking specific children’s sleeping bags for them – if there’s too much space in a sleeping bag, they can be harder to keep warm.

You’ll also want to remember pillows (you can get lightweight, packable ones for camping), something to sleep in, ear plugs (for the snorers in the next tent) and an eye mask (for the early morning light, unless your tent has a blackout bedroom).

A word of warning. Even in the midst of a heatwave, your tent will get cool in the night, so don’t be tempted to rely on a blanket or super thin sleeping bag. Oh, and make sure you pack plenty of warm layers for lazing around camp, too!

Camp kitchen equipment

© lfto/matt jones

You can pick and choose here. You can easily keep this minimal if you plan to eat at a pub every night, or if there’s a pizza truck or something similar on site, in which case you only need to worry about breakfast (get pastries and cereal bars for a low-effort brekkie) and lunch.

If you intend to cook then you’ll need a camping stove (or a barbeque, if you’d prefer that) – ideally a double burner if you’re cooking for the whole family, though wide single burners can work if you’ll only be cooking one-pot wonders.

Then you’ll need the correct gas (check it has the right connection), a lighter or matches, pots or pans, cooking utensils, plates or bowls, cutlery and potentially a tin opener, depending on what you’re cooking.

Don’t forget little essentials like cooking oil, salt and pepper and sauces (stock up on sachets when you’re at hotels or fast food chains).

A camping fridge or a cool box are essential if you’re doing lots of cooking, while you’ll also want to take some form of washing up bowl, washing up liquid and a tea towel.

And finally, if you have electric hook-up, you may want to take a small toaster or kettle, though your camping stove can also be used for both.

Lighting

© lfto/tom bailey

Make sure you pack some form of camping light or lantern to keep things lit up at night, while a couple of head torches will help you navigate the campsite (and your tent) without tripping over guy lines.

Camp furniture

Camping chairs and a camping table are pretty essential if you want everyone to enjoy the camping trip – otherwise, you’ll be sat on the cold, hard floor.

Think about what type of table you need and how easily it’ll fit into your car. Do you plan to eat at a table or on your lap? Would a small table work to cook on and use as a coffee table? Or do you need something bigger?

Other essentials

© lfto/milo wilson

You also won’t want to forget bin bags, spare toilet roll, wet wipes, a bottle opener, spare batteries, personal medication, a first aid kit, sun cream, anti-bac handwash, insect repellent and a multitool. I tend to keep that latter item to hand in the tent through the night so it can be used to create a quick exit through the flysheet in case of an emergency (such as a fire).

I’d also recommend packing a lot of these smaller items into a couple of storage boxes. It helps with transport, but also makes it easier for you to keep the tent organised throughout your trip – there’s nothing worse than everything being spread out all over the place.

Don’t forget your toiletry bags and towels, either, while a pair of flip flops per person is incredibly handy for late-night toilet trips or shower runs.

Oh, and don’t forget to take water bottles so you can stay hydrated throughout the day.

Optional extras

© lfto/tom bailey

Some people will be tempted to take a portable toilet to avoid late-night walks to the bathroom block, though this isn’t an essential.

A windbreak can be helpful for added privacy and keeping your seating/cooking area protected on a windy day, but you can easily get by without one.

You may want to take a brush to help keep your tent nice and clean, while spare tent pegs can be useful if you think you don’t have enough or the ones you do have aren’t very good.

A lot of people take a water container camping with them, which can be handy to save you from repeated trips to the drinking tap, but if you’re not on a very large campsite, you may not have to walk far anyway.

Finally, you’ll probably want to pack a couple of games to help occupy everyone – a football, frisbee, Uno or Dobble all work well.

Take the stress out

© go outdoors

Natalie Wolfenden, author and outdoor enthusiast at GO Outdoors, said: “Camping has long been one of the most accessible and rewarding ways for families to spend quality time together outdoors, but a little preparation can make a huge difference to the overall experience. Having a clear packing checklist helps take the stress out of getting away, ensuring you have the essentials covered while avoiding unnecessary last-minute purchases or forgotten items.

“When camping with children, comfort and practicality are often the key considerations. Choosing the right sleeping kit, preparing for changing weather conditions and planning simple meal solutions can help families feel more confident and make the most of their time outside.

“The beauty of family camping is that it doesn’t need to be complicated. With the right equipment and a bit of forward planning, families can focus less on logistics and more on creating lasting memories together in the outdoors.”
