MONDAY UPDATE: Fires in Crook County growing but containment progress being made

Published 10:48 am Monday, July 27, 2026

By Jason Chaney

Task forces arrive as firefighting efforts continue. (Oregon Department of Forestery/submitted)

Wildfires near Prineville and in eastern Crook County continue to grow, but firefighters are making containment progress on some of them.

Coyote and Henry fires

The Coyote Fire, (17,447 acres, 57% contained) and Henry Fire (28,827 acres, 35% contained) both continue to burn in the Post and Paulina area as unique challenges continue to arise.

An unstable air mass over the Coyote and Henry fires is expected to cause active fire behavior and the possibility of dust devils forming, officials said. Dust devil formation can happen because of the temperature difference between the surface and the air. This raises a concern for firefighters because it can cause spotting and move fire quickly from one location to another and appear as smoke because of the ash from the fire.

Firefighters will continue to patrol and secure the western edge of the Coyote fire. Heading down to the southwest perimeter, crews are using dozer lines and handlines to build direct containment lines along the fire. Progress is being made along the eastern edge, with containment lines being secured overnight, and firefighters will continue to mop up. Crews and aircraft will remain vigilant and available to assist with any new starts or calls for assistance.

The Henry Fire’s increased containment is from the grueling hard work the firefighters have done on the eastern edge, officials reported. The containment lines on the east and north side of the fire have been strengthened and continued to be patrolled and mopped up. The possibility of interior smoldering and isolated torching on unburnt interior vegetation remains a concern.

The southeast perimeter saw no movement out of the fire’s footprint. The fire did spot over South Fork Creek on the western fire edge. Firefighters stopped forward progress by utilizing hose lays and direct suppression tactics. This spot fire was mopped up by evening shifts and will continue today. In the southwest portion of the fire, a hotshot crew is hiking through rugged, steep terrain to access an area that still has residual heat and to stop the fire’s movement to the south. A helitack crew will be assisting the hotshot crew in this area of the fire.

Brewer, Crosswhite and Green Mountain fires

As of Monday morning, the Brewer Fire was 51,308 acres and 5% contained. The Crosswhite Fire had reached 191,941 acres and was 31% contained, and the Green Mountain Fire was 2,136 acres and 0% contained.

Firefighters continue to battle the fires, near Prineville, as they head into their 13th day. Crews continue to make progress, halting western progression and holding lines just north of Prineville.

Firefighters continue to patrol and mop up the south-southwest portion of the Brewer Fire and are strengthening control lines by removing any vegetation that could ignite. Sunday night, firefighters used unmanned aerial systems (UAS) to confirm that the fire remained secure within the Lofton and McKay Creek areas. To the northeast, firefighters were able to get around the majority of fire that crossed lines along the A-Y Road and continued securing those areas and improved control lines.

Sunday night, heat over the fire was so intense that aviation was unable to fly over the incident to map fire growth with infrared. The incident will post new acreage when available. Suppression aircraft will be in use today throughout all three fires as available.

On the southern portion of the Green Mountain Fire, fire activity has been slowly backing down the mountain toward North Mill Creek Road. Crews are constructing a combination of dozer and handline, connecting North Mill Creek and McKay Creek roads and will continue strengthening those lines as the fire meets them.

On the Crosswhite West Fire, crews continue to perform structure protection on homes in the Marks Creek area while also constructing indirect line to secure the fire from crossing the highway. Sunday night, resources from Crosswhite West assisted Crosswhite East, securing several spot fires that had crossed Highway 26. On the northern portion of the fire, crews continue to perform prep work on indirect line north of Stephenson Mountain.

While the Red Flag Warning was lifted, firefighters still expect hot, dry and windy conditions on the fireline. Columns are expected to be visible again today, with the inversion lifting late morning to early afternoon.

Highway and public lands closures

As of Sunday, U.S. Highway 26 is closed east of Prineville at milepost 38 to the John Day River, at the junction with OR Highway 19 at milepost 98.

Current Ochoco National Forest closures, which can be found at fs.usda.gov/r06/ochoco/alerts/brewer-and-box-springs-fire-area-closure, include all lands, roads and trails to the west side of Forest Service Road (FSR) 22, extending to the northern forest boundary on FSR 2630. Highway 26 is not affected by the closure as of Saturday evening.

Additionally, the east side of the Ochoco National Forest is closed from the southern forest boundary on the east side of FSR 4240, extending north on FSR 3010, to the northern forest boundary on FSR 22.

Closures also encompass nearly all BLM-administered lands in the Prineville District, including broad areas from the Columbia River south to Mitchell, west to the Deschutes National Forest boundary, north around Lake Billy Chinook, the Lower Deschutes River corridor near Warm Springs and lands extending from Interstate 84 south through Madras, Prineville and Highway 20 to the district’s eastern boundary.
