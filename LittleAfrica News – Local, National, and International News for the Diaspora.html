South Carolina Chosen to Lead Democrats’ 2028 Presidential Primary Calendar

by Michelle Wilson
July 26, 2026
in U.S.

South Carolina will become the first state to vote in the Democratic presidential nomination process for 2028, marking a major shift in the party’s primary calendar. 

The Democratic National Committee’s Rules and Bylaws Committee selected South Carolina to hold the opening contest, a decision that must still receive final approval from the full DNC.

The move reflects Democrats’ effort to give greater influence to states with diverse electorates, particularly Black voters who make up a key part of the party’s base. 

Under the proposed 2028 schedule, South Carolina would hold its primary on January 22, followed by Nevada, New Hampshire, New Mexico, Michigan, and Virginia before Super Tuesday.

The decision sparked debate within the party, with some leaders arguing Nevada’s growing Latino population deserved the first slot. Critics also questioned whether the new calendar could favor certain candidates over others.

The reshuffling ends Iowa’s longtime role as the opening contest and signals a new approach to selecting the Democratic nominee.
